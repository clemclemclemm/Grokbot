The decision

Treat wage intensity and CapEx mix as one board pack this quarter. Measure cost per head and buildings-versus-equipment before you approve another quiet CapEx year.

What changed

Three signals (access 2026-09-23):

Brief 1 · Wages vs headcount (ABS). Manufacturing wages and salaries +5.2% ($3.8b) in FY 2024-25; employment +0.2% (+2,000); end-June 899,000 → 901,000 (Source fact, ABS Australian Industry). Derived: wage intensity per head about +5.0% ((1.052 / 1.002) - 1 ≈ 0.0499). Same release: IVA +4.6% to $141.973b; sales +6.9%; expenses +7.8%; EBITDA +1.6% ($753m) (Source fact, current-price). Food earnings (+7.8%, $815m) drive much of division EBITDA (context only).

Brief 2 · Siemens / P&G line inspection. Visual Inspection Cockpit (VIC): scrap reduced 10 to 20% (product-dependent); deployments 5 to 10x faster than bespoke vision (Source fact: OEM + customer joint claim, WebWire Siemens, 17 Sep 2026). Stack: P&G models on Siemens Industrial Edge + Nvidia GPU PCs; PLC reject (Source fact: architecture).

Brief 3 · CapEx buildings vs equipment (ABS). Manufacturing CapEx -5.0% YoY Jun qtr 2026 vs Jun qtr 2025; buildings -16.2%; equipment +1.6% YoY / +0.8% QoQ (Source fact, ABS Private New CapEx, SA chain volumes). Keep all-industry ICT swings out of this manuf story.

Why it matters

PME boss, plant chef, and fund share one squeeze: the wage bill climbs about twenty-six times faster than headcount while expenses outrun sales and EBITDA barely lifts. That is cost-intensity and automation ROI, not a shortage slogan.

CapEx rooms that wait for a building cycle may miss a window where kit is flat-to-up while shells contract. Vision that cuts scrap only helps if sized to your line, not to a joint press range.

What could make this wrong

One limit: scrap 10 to 20% and commissioning 5 to 10x are a Siemens + P&G joint claim (channel / customer-reported), not an independent trial or an Australian plant norm.

Three questions for the room

1.What is wage cost per head (or per unit) by shift for the last four quarters, restated the same way?
2.In the next CapEx pack, are you funding a shell, funding kit, or freezing both while wage intensity rises?
3.Which inspection or reject loop has a dated scrap series you own before you buy another vision stack?

Recommended next move (90 days)

1.Days 1-3: name one unit (wage intensity, scrap escapes, or kit vs shell) and one owner.
2.Days 4-30: baseline finance can restate; separate Source fact from vendor claim.
3.Days 31-60: one-page note; size process, kit, or inspection pilot to the baseline only.
4.Days 61-90: run or refuse; no second unit before the first baseline exists.

Short reportage

Manufacturing is paying more per head while headcount barely moves. Boards that only hear shortage or soft demand miss the ABS print: wages +5.2%, employment +0.2%, expenses ahead of sales, EBITDA barely up. Automation ROI starts there.

The CapEx split is the second half of the same pack. Buildings -16.2% while equipment +1.6% means shells are contracting faster than kit. Waiting for a building cycle is a choice. So is buying kit against a dated bottleneck while wage intensity climbs.

Siemens/P&G VIC is pressure, not the thesis: cameras and industrial AI rejecting at PLC speed with claimed scrap cuts. Useful only after your scrap series exists.
