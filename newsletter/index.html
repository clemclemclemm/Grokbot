Six numbers you rarely see

Industrial robots (factory arms), density, China scale, elec vs auto, AU AI by size, IVA lift, thin GDP share. Sourced. Then a hard close.

Industrial robot density is rising, factory demand is shifting, and Australia's manufacturing slice of GDP stays thin. Six dated figures from IFR, ABS and the World Bank. One question at the end: are throughput plans still built for last year's plant?

01 · World density 177 / 10k World density

What we mean by robot. an industrial robot, typically a fixed or mobile articulated arm on a factory floor (welding, handling, assembly, palletising), not a phone, vacuum, or chat tool.

Global density rose to 177 industrial robots per 10,000 people employed in manufacturing in 2024, from 163 the year before (IFR World Robotics 2025 Foreword). In the same year, new installations reached 542,076 (+0.1%) while the operational stock hit 4,663,773 (+8.9%). Top densities: Korea 1,220, Singapore 818, China 567, Germany 449, Japan 446.

The odd pair is the point. Fresh orders barely moved; the installed base kept growing. Plants that already automated are still running that kit. Plants that waited did not get a "pause year." They got a higher bar.

CapEx talk that still treats line automation as exceptional is arguing with a world average that already moved. A quieter year on new orders is not the same as a quieter competitive floor.

Use 177 as a baseline for competitive manufacturing, then check where each site sits against it.

Sources IFR World Robotics 2025 Foreword (calendar year 2024).

02 · China vs world 3.2× China vs world

China's manufacturing robot density is 567 per 10,000 employees, about 3.2x the world average of 177 (same IFR year). China also took 54% of global deployments in 2024 (~295,045 installations), with operational stock above 2,027,000. Domestic suppliers held 57% of that market (from about 28% a decade earlier; 47% to 57% year-on-year).

Density and deployment share together describe a learning climate: more plants on repeatable processes, more maintenance skill, more vendors competing on uptime.

the useful response is not "copy China." It is stopping the habit of using "we're not China" to delay sequenced capital, a named floor owner, and metrics finance will sign. Plants in audited supply chains will meet that climate as tighter specs and less patience on variance.

Triple world density plus half of new deployments is an operating climate. Price the delay, or stop calling delay prudence.

Sources IFR World Robotics 2025 Foreword; IFR press 25 Sep 2025 (calendar year 2024).

03 · Elec over auto Elec > Auto Elec over auto

In 2024 electronics again led automotive on new industrial robot installations: 128,899 vs 126,088 (gap ~2,800). Shares of global installs: electronics 24% (+1 pp), automotive 23% (-2 pp), metal/machinery 16% (+2 pp). Fourth straight year above 500,000 new installs worldwide. Regional split of new deployments: Asia 74%, Europe 16%, Americas 9% (IFR WR 2025 Exec Summary; press 25 Sep 2025).

The gap is only ~2,800 units: a razor handover, not an electronics tidal wave. The story is where flexible, high-mix, high-scrutiny lines are pulling budget.

capital plans still anchored to a 2015 automotive mental model will mis-read demand. Follow where new arms are ordered, not where the longest plant histories sit.

In 2024 the install lead moved to electronics, with Asia still dominating new deployments.

Sources IFR World Robotics 2025 Foreword / Exec Summary; IFR press 25 Sep 2025 (calendar year 2024).

04 · AI size gap 3.2× AI size gap

About 12% of Australian businesses used AI in 2024-25. Large firms ~35%, small/micro ~11% (~3.2x), medium ~22% (vs 3% in 2021-22). Large firms rose from ~9% to 35% over that stretch. Innovation-active businesses: 20% AI use vs 6% for others. 46% of businesses were innovation-active. Top sectors included info/media/telecom 38%, professional scientific and finance 24% each (ABS media 25 Jun 2026; FY 2024-25 BCS).

This is a market-structure chart more than a tech chart. Adoption tracks firm size and innovation activity, not slogans.

the failure mode is buying a licence, skipping process redesign, and expecting scrap or inspection time to move. Finance should demand units it can audit: scrap avoided, inspection minutes, unplanned downtime, shift handoffs. Without those units, spend is fashion.

Australia's AI pattern is a size split. Medium at ~22% shows the middle is already moving.

Sources ABS Business Characteristics Survey / media 25 Jun 2026 (FY 2024-25).

05 · AU IVA lift +A$6.2bn AU IVA lift

Manufacturing industry value added rose about A$6.2 billion between 2023-24 and 2024-25 (A$141,973m after A$135,724m), +4.6%. Employment end of June ~901,000 (+0.2%). Value and headcount edged up together.

That undercuts a pure decline narrative. It does not prove lines are under control.

reading the lift as permission to delay upgrades is the trap. Rising IVA can hide ageing assets, overtime treated as normal, and quality variance customers price later. Use the cash-flow window for sensing, data, and robotics while it still exists.

manufacturing still adds value and jobs at the margin. The open question is how much value sits behind outdated lines kept out of habit.

Sources ABS Australian Industry 2024-25 (FY window; employment end June).

06 · Thin GDP share 5.4% Thin GDP share

Manufacturing was about 5.4% of Australian GDP in 2024, versus 18.0% in Germany and 24.8% in China (World Bank NV.IND.MANF.ZS). Structural, not moral: resources, services, geography.

Context from IFR press 25 Sep 2025: Europe installed ~85,006 industrial robots in 2024 (-8%), EU ~80% of that, Germany 26,982 (-5%, ~32% of Europe). Thick bases still cycle investment; they do not abandon control.

a thin manufacturing share means less volume to absorb waste. One unreliable line becomes a customer risk faster. "Good enough" without a measured unit is a soft plan.

keep the lines worth keeping, and modernise them; retire the ones defended only by habit.

Sources World Bank NV.IND.MANF.ZS (2024); IFR press 25 Sep 2025 (Europe install context).

This week's take

Run the plants you have

Position. Australia does not win by mimicking Germany or China. It wins by running the plants it keeps with less waste, faster feedback, and automation finance can audit.

Facts (one pass)

World density moved 163 to 177 while operational stock grew +8.9% even as new installs barely moved (+0.1%, IFR World Robotics 2025 Foreword (calendar year 2024)). That is the stock-versus-flow split: the installed base keeps working while new orders look calm. China runs ~3.2x world density and took 54% of 2024 deployments. Electronics edged past automotive on new installs by only ~2,800 units (128,899 vs 126,088); Asia took 74% of new deployments. In Australia, AI use splits by size (large ~35%, medium ~22%, small/micro ~11%). Manufacturing IVA rose ~A$6.2bn with jobs slightly up, on 5.4% of GDP. Europe still installed ~85,006 industrial robots in 2024 (-8%).

flat global installs plus a rising stock, and a razor-thin elec-over-auto gap, are the measured backdrop, not a boom headline.

Opinion (single block)

the market is not "exploding"; the base already installed is compounding while late movers wait for a quieter year that does not reset the competitive floor. The elec-over-auto lead is a knife-edge handover, not an electronics tidal wave, which is enough to retire a 2015 automotive mental model for CapEx. Optional upgrades feel safe until scrap, changeover, or downtime shows up in the quote. CapEx lists that reshuffle without a baseline, a floor owner, and a payback unit finance accepts are drift. A thin manufacturing share of GDP does not get Europe's industrial cushion; budgeting as if it does is how margin leaks. Trust is dated sources and a hard split between fact and reading. Passion is a line that behaves the same on night shift. Fun is ops and finance sharing one chart. Theatre is a cell without a metric, an AI licence without a redesigned handoff, or a strategy day that never names scrap, changeover, or downtime. Put one unit on the leadership agenda before any vendor meeting (worst rework SKU, Friday bottleneck changeover, or named downtime asset), run it thirty to sixty days with the people on the line, then choose capital. No baseline, no pilot. No vendor-owned IP by default. No upgrade that cannot survive a board question in labour hours, scrap, or recovered capacity. If throughput assumptions are still built for last year's plant, pick one unit this week with an owner and a date; if not, fund the line you already know. "Later" is how optional upgrades become margin leakage.
