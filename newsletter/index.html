Six numbers you rarely see

World to Australia. Density, demand mix, AI by firm size, IVA lift, thin GDP share: sourced, dated, no pitch up front.

Mid-market plants in Australia still treat some floor upgrades as optional. Competitors who already run tighter density, faster changeovers, or cleaner inspection do not. Below are six dated figures from IFR, ABS and the World Bank, then a plain question for ops and finance: are your throughput assumptions still built for last year's plant?

01 · World density 177 / 10k World density

Global robot density rose again in 2024: 177 industrial robots for every 10,000 people employed in manufacturing, up from 163 the year before. That figure is a factory baseline, not a gadget story.

The same IFR Foreword frame shows how busy that floor stayed. New installations worldwide reached542,076in 2024 (+0.1% versus 2023), while the operational stock hit4,663,773(+8.9%). New orders can stall while the installed base keeps running. Top densities in the same year: Korea1,220, Singapore818, China567, Germany449, Japan446per 10,000 manufacturing employees.

Opinion if CapEx talks still treat automation as exceptional, they are out of date. The world average already moved. Waiting for a quieter year is how mid-market sites lock in last year's throughput.

Opinion for Australian ops and finance leaders, the useful question is not whether robots exist. It is whether your labour and throughput assumptions still match a world that is re-tooling while you reshuffle the same CapEx list.

Takeaway Treat 177 as normal for competitive manufacturing. Then answer, without a workshop: where do your sites sit relative to that floor?

Sources IFR World Robotics 2025 Foreword (calendar year 2024).

02 · China vs world 3.2× China vs world

China's manufacturing robot density is567per 10,000 employees. Against the world average of 177, that is roughly3.2x. Same year. Same IFR frame.

Scale shows up in deployments too. In 2024 China accounted for54%of global robot deployments, about295,045installations (a record). Its operational stock sat above2,027,000. Domestic suppliers took57%of that market (up from about28%a decade earlier; year-on-year the domestic share moved47% to 57%).

Opinion copying China is not the brief. Using "we're not China" to delay upgrades is. A 3.2x density gap plus half of world deployments is an operating climate. Mid-market AU plants in audited supply chains will meet it as tighter specs, less patience on variance, and quotes that already price lower scrap risk.

Opinion a 3.2x gap is not closed with a one-off pilot. It needs sequenced capital, a named owner on the floor, and metrics finance will sign.

Takeaway When a peer economy runs at triple world density and takes over half of new deployments, "we'll revisit after peak season" is a strategy with a cost. Name the cost or stop pretending it is prudence.

Sources IFR World Robotics 2025 Foreword; IFR press 25 Sep 2025 (calendar year 2024).

03 · Elec over auto Elec > Auto Elec over auto

In 2024, electronics again overtook automotive as the larger buyer of new industrial robot installations,128,899against126,088(gap about2,800units). As shares of global installs, electronics was24%(+1 percentage point) and automotive23%(-2 pp). Metal and machinery sat third at16%(+2 pp). It was the fourth straight year above500,000new installations worldwide. By region, Asia took74%of new deployments, Europe16%, the Americas9%.

Automation follows the supply chains that are moving, not only the plants with the longest history.

Opinion if your mental model of "serious robotics" is still 2015 automotive, you will mis-read where budget is going. High-mix, high-scrutiny, short-rework-tolerance lines are where the install story moved.

Opinion that is where computer vision and disciplined robotics earn their keep, not as theatre, as throughput insurance.

Takeaway Follow where new robots are actually ordered. In 2024, electronics edged past auto, and Asia still dominated new deployments. Plan capital against that map, not nostalgia.

Sources IFR World Robotics 2025 Foreword / Exec Summary; IFR press 25 Sep 2025 (calendar year 2024).

04 · AI size gap 3.2× AI size gap

Only about12%of Australian businesses used AI in 2024-25. Dig one layer deeper: roughly35%of large firms versus about11%of small and micro. That is ~3.2x. Medium firms sat near~22%(versus3%in 2021-22). Large firms moved from about9%to35%over that stretch. Innovation-active businesses used AI at20%versus6%for the rest.46%of businesses were innovation-active. Top sectors included information/media/telecom at38%, and professional scientific services and finance at24%each (ABS media 25 Jun 2026; FY 2024-25 BCS window).

Opinion mid-market sits in the hard middle of the ABS split: too complex to ignore AI tools, too lean for empty pilots. The usual failure is buying a licence, skipping process redesign, calling it done, then watching scrap stay flat.

Opinion for finance, the unit that matters is not "AI spend". It is avoided scrap, faster inspection, fewer unplanned stops, cleaner handoffs between shifts. If those units are not in the business case, you are funding fashion while large peers compound process advantage.

Takeaway Australia's AI story is a size split. Medium at ~22% means the middle is already moving. Waiting for certainty is how you watch the 35% club pull away.

Sources ABS Business Characteristics Survey / media 25 Jun 2026 (FY 2024-25).

05 · AU IVA lift +A$6.2bn AU IVA lift

Australian manufacturing industry value added rose by aboutA$6.2 billionin absolute terms between 2023-24 and 2024-25 (A$141,973m after A$135,724m), a+4.6%lift. Employment at end of June sat near901,000, up0.2%on the prior year. Value and headcount edged up together.

Opinion manufacturing in Australia is not finished. It still adds value and labour. The risk is reading the IVA lift as permission to delay upgrades. Rising IVA can hide ageing assets, overtime treated as normal, and quality variance customers will price later.

Opinion use the lift to fund the boring upgrades, sensing, data, robotics, while cash flow still supports them. Finance should like the ABS transparency. Ops should stop saying "there is nothing left to improve here."

Takeaway AU manufacturing still adds value and jobs at the margin. The open question on your site is how much of that value is trapped behind outdated lines you keep calling "good enough."

Sources ABS Australian Industry 2024-25 (FY window; employment end June).

06 · Thin GDP share 5.4% Thin GDP share

Manufacturing is about5.4%of Australian GDP (2024). Germany sits near18.0%; China near24.8%. We are not a factory economy. That is structural: resources, services, geography. Not a moral failing.

Even thick factory economies invest in cycles. In 2024 Europe installed about85,006industrial robots (-8%), of which the EU was roughly80%. Germany alone installed26,982(-5%), about32%of Europe's total.

Opinion thin share is not permission to be sloppy. It is the opposite. Less volume means less cushion. One unreliable line becomes a customer risk faster here than in a factory-heavy economy. If your mental benchmark is "good enough for Australia," you are using geography as a pillow. That pillow is thinner than a site tour suggests.

Opinion Australia can stay high-skill and high-mix. It cannot afford loose control on the lines it keeps.

Takeaway Little manufacturing share of GDP means little room for sloppy throughput. Modernise the lines you intend to keep. Retire the ones you are only defending out of habit.

Sources World Bank NV.IND.MANF.ZS (2024); IFR press 25 Sep 2025 for Europe install context.

This week's take

Run the plants you have

Position / Opinion:Australia will not win by pretending it is Germany or China. Mid-market wins by running the plants it keeps with less waste, faster feedback, and automation finance can audit. Soft framing is how CapEx lists reshuffle forever without retiring a bad idea.

The six numbers as one argument (aimed at you)

Opinion read these as one argument in sequence, not a chart dump.

World density rose from 163 to 177 while operational stock grew+8.9%even as new installs barely moved (IFR World Robotics 2025 Foreword, calendar 2024). China runs about 3.2x world density and took54%of 2024 deployments (IFR press 25 Sep 2025). Electronics edged past automotive on new installs, withAsia at 74%of new deployments. At home, AI splits by size: large near 35%, medium near22%, small/micro near 11% (ABS BCS, FY 2024-25). Manufacturing IVA still grew about A$6.2 billion, employment slightly up, on only 5.4% of GDP (ABS; World Bank 2024).

Fact stitch Asia's 74% share of new installs, world stock +8.9%, and medium-firm AI at ~22% in Australia are the measured backdrop.

Opinion for mid-market leaders, the early-adopter story is over. Medium-firm AI near 22% already shows movement. If the optional-upgrade habit still feels safe, test it against your worst-performing line.

What "optional" is costing your P&L

Opinion optional automation is not free. It is overtime that became culture, inspection that catches defects after value is trapped in the part, and quotes that lose because scrap risk sits in the price. Finance sees it late as margin noise. Ops sees it every Friday. The gap between those clocks is where "prudent delay" lives.

Opinion the pattern is familiar. Ops raises a pain point. Finance asks for a business case. Ops brings a vendor deck. Finance asks for a baseline. Nobody owns the baseline, so another workshop is booked. Months later the same cell is still the bottleneck. That is drift, not control.

Thin GDP share (5.4% versus Germany 18.0% and China 24.8%) means less volume and less cushion. Europe still installed ~85,006 robots in 2024 (-8%): deep bases adjust timing rather than abandoning upgrades.

Opinion Australian mid-market does not get Europe's industrial cushion. Stop budgeting as if it does.

Trust, passion, fun without the brochure voice

Opinion trust here means dated sources, transparent ratios, and a hard split between fact and opinion. If a claim cannot carry source, date, and window, it does not sit beside ABS or IFR.

Opinion passion is care for the line: changeovers that do not invent defects, inspection that catches drift early, downtime with an owner. It is refusing to celebrate a pilot that never named a unit.

Opinion fun, for industrial B2B, is night shift matching day shift, and ops and finance pointing at the same chart without a translator. That is closer to what executives mean when they say they buy ROI.

Opinion boards fail by buying theatre: a robot cell without a metric, an AI licence without a redesigned handoff, a strategy day that never names scrap, changeover, or downtime. If the unit is not on the page, the capital story is fashion.

How mid-market gets priced (this is the part about you)

Opinion denser automation abroad and Australia's size-split AI pattern will not skip you. They will price you. Slower quotes. Tighter customer audits. Less forgiveness on variance. Copying China (54% of deployments, domestic suppliers at 57%) is not the brief. Keeping the plants worth keeping, and making their feedback loops boringly good, is.

Opinion medium AI near 22% means the peer set is already moving. Large firms near 35% are not waiting. Customers who audit quality systems will not pause for a roadmap that says "after peak." Peak is when variance hurts most.

Opinion the question is shifting from "do we have robots?" to "can we prove the line is under control?" Proof is boring. Proof renews contracts. Soft language about "exploring options" does not.

What you should do this quarter (if it were my call)

Opinion if I sat in your leadership meeting, I would force one unit onto the whiteboard before any vendor enters the building. Not "quality." First-pass yield on the SKU that burns the most rework hours. Not "efficiency." Changeover minutes on the cell that gatekeeps Friday despatch. Not "digital." Unplanned downtime on the asset that already has a nickname. If it needs a glossary, it is not ready for CapEx.

Opinion then instrument that unit so ops and finance both trust the number. Improve it thirty to sixty days with the people who run the line. Only then decide what capital follows (sensing, vision, robotics, or process). That order beats another Industry 4.0 slide.

Opinion say no early. No baseline, no pilot. No vendor-owned IP by default. No upgrade that cannot survive a board question in labour hours, scrap, or recovered capacity. Vendor-agnostic work and client-held IP are how trust survives procurement, not decoration.

Opinion if a proposal cannot survive three questions (baseline, floor owner, payback unit finance accepts), it is not a modernisation plan. It is a purchase order with optimism attached. Kill it.

Yesterday's plant (look in the mirror)

Opinion yesterday's plant is not always old paint. Sometimes it is a clean floor with tribal knowledge in three people, a quality gate in a spreadsheet, and a CapEx list that reshuffles without ever dying: strong revenue, soft process control. The IFR and ABS numbers do not accuse a postcode; they set the climate.

Opinion "good enough for Australia" is not a plan. With manufacturing at 5.4% of GDP, there is less volume to absorb waste than a tidy site tour suggests.

Close: the hook, earned

We opened on a practical gap: mid-market plants still treating some upgrades as optional while denser, tighter competitors do not. The numbers did not soften that. Density 163 to 177, stock +8.9%. China at ~3.2x density and ~half of deployments. Electronics past auto; Asia at 74% of new installs. AU AI split with medium near 22%. IVA up ~A$6.2bn on 5.4% of GDP.

Same question, now earned: are your throughput assumptions still built for last year's plant?

Opinion if yes, pick one unit this week and put it on the leadership agenda with an owner and a date. If no, you already know which line to fund. "Later" is not a plan. Delay is how optional upgrades become margin leakage, and how mid-market sites lose ground to plants that already treat control as normal.

Opinion write the answer in one sentence you would put in a board pack. If it needs hedging, the cost of delay is already visible on the floor.
